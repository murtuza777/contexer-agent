# How professional engineers ship production code with AI coding agents (state as of Oct 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Labels on every claim:
- **[VENDOR]**: the tool maker describing its own product or customers.
- **[CO-SELF]**: a company describing its own internal use, which is not independently audited.
- **[PRACTITIONER]**: an independent engineer's first-hand account.
- **[SECONDARY]**: an aggregator, press summary or third-party write-up. Verify these against the primary source before relying on them.
- **[RESEARCH]**: a survey or study.

Scope note: the web-search budget ran out partway through. Coverage of Google Jules, Devin, Factory, Amp, Kiro, Tessl, task-master, Sculptor, Terragon, Vercel, Linear, Airbnb and Block is therefore thinner than coverage of Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Copilot. ## Q0. End-to-end: which steps (idea → spec → execution → test → review → merge → deploy) are autonomous today and which are babysat?

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the middle of the loop is routinely unattended: agent execution plus self-testing in a sandbox, followed by a first-pass AI review. That holds both at companies and for heavy individual users. The two ends remain human-gated: deciding *what* to build (spec/plan approval) and accepting *what ships* (PR review/merge, release). Fully unattended review and merge exist only as deliberate experiments (OpenAI's harness-engineering team, StrongDM's "software factory") or in narrow, highly verifiable domains (fleet-wide migrations, flaky-test fixes).

### Cited Findings
**Idea / spec (mostly human, increasingly agent-drafted)**
- [PRACTITIONER, Every, 2025-12-11] Every's "compound engineering" loop is Plan → Work → Review → Compound. "Roughly 80 percent of compound engineering is in the plan and review parts." Agents research the codebase and write the plan, but planning "requires the developer to think hard." — [Every](https://every.to/source-code/compound-engineering-how-every-codes-with-agents)
- [CO-SELF, Anthropic, 2025-12-02] Anthropic engineers keep "high-level thinking, strategy, and design" and decisions that need "taste" or organizational context. More than half said they could "fully delegate" only 0–20% of their work. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)
- [VENDOR, GitHub, 2025-09-02] Spec Kit puts explicit human checkpoints between Specify → Plan → Tasks → Implement: "The AI generates the artifacts; you ensure they're right." — [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/generative-ai/spec-driven-development-with-ai-get-started-with-a-new-open-source-toolkit/)

**Execution (largely autonomous within a sandbox)**
- [CO-SELF, Stripe, 2026-02-09] Minions are "one-shot, end-to-end" unattended agents. They run in pre-warmed devboxes (spin-up ~10s) that are isolated from production and the internet, which "allows runs without human permission checks." "Over a thousand pull requests merged each week" are completely minion-produced. — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents)
- [CO-SELF via VENDOR, Ramp/Modal, 2026-02-19] Ramp's Inspect runs each session in a Modal sandbox with the full stack (Postgres, Redis, Temporal, RabbitMQ), a browser and tests. "Roughly half of all merged PRs across Ramp's frontend and backend repos were started by Inspect." — [Modal](https://modal.com/blog/how-ramp-built-a-full-context-background-coding-agent-on-modal)
- [CO-SELF, Anthropic, 2025-12-02] Between Feb and Aug 2025, max consecutive tool calls per Claude Code transcript rose from 9.8 to 21.2 (+116%), and human turns per transcript fell from 6.2 to 4.1 (−33%). — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)

**Testing (autonomous when the harness supplies it)**
- [CO-SELF, Stripe, 2026-02-09] On each push a local lint runs in under 5 seconds. CI then runs selected tests from more than 3 million, with autofixes. The minion gets at most two CI rounds and then stops. — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents)
- [CO-SELF, Ramp, 2026-02-19] Inspect "takes before-and-after screenshots and navigates the app in a real browser" to confirm frontend changes. — [Modal](https://modal.com/blog/how-ramp-built-a-full-context-background-coding-agent-on-modal)

**Review (first pass by AI, final by humans almost everywhere)**
- [CO-SELF, Stripe, 2026-03-20] "All changes are human-reviewed but contain no human-written code." — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/stripe-autonomous-coding-agents/)
- [VENDOR, GitHub, 2025-10-28] The Copilot coding agent now runs "a first-pass code review step before the developer sees its code." — [GitHub](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/welcome-home-agents/)
- [CO-SELF, Spotify, 2025-11-06] Honk PRs go through Spotify's unchanged human review and merge processes, after an LLM-as-judge step evaluates each diff. — [Spotify Engineering](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2025/11/spotifys-background-coding-agent-part-1)

**Merge / deploy (human- or CI-gated; autonomous only in experiments)**
- [CO-SELF via SECONDARY, OpenAI, 2026] On OpenAI's harness-engineering team, agents push PRs through the merge queue with a "$land" skill. The summary of the talk says there is zero human review before merge and that humans review samples after merge. Elsewhere it describes a human making binary mergeable/not-mergeable calls twice a day. "Release branches require human-approved smoke tests." — [ZenML summary of Ryan Lopopolo talk](https://zenml.io/llmops-database/extreme-harness-engineering-building-production-software-with-zero-human-written-code)
- [CO-SELF via PRACTITIONER, StrongDM, 2026-02-07] StrongDM's rules are "Code must not be written by humans. Code must not be reviewed by humans." Verification comes from holdout "scenarios" and a "Digital Twin Universe" of cloned SaaS dependencies. They budget roughly $1,000/engineer/day in tokens. Simon Willison questions how the no-review rule can be sensible. — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/7/software-factory/)
- [PRACTITIONER, Peter Steinberger, ~Oct 2025] In his own projects, a PR deploys a preview in about 2 minutes, and an `/automerge` command handles bot comments and squashes once CI is green. — [steipete.me](https://steipete.me/posts/just-talk-to-it)

### Inferences
- Autonomy maps onto *verifiability*: agents run unattended where a machine-checkable oracle exists (tests, CI, type checks, screenshots, a GCC oracle). Humans stay in the loop where correctness depends on intent, taste or accountability.
- "Deploy" is the least agentified step in the evidence. Every company account ends at "PR ready for human review" or a human-approved release gate. I found no mainstream company claiming agents deploy to production unsupervised.

### Gaps
- No primary source quantifies how often agent PRs are merged *without modification* at any company. Uber's summary explicitly notes this is unreported.
- I found no company engineering post describing agents performing production deploys or rollbacks autonomously.

## Q1. How do engineers plan and specify work for agents?

### Takeaway
There are two camps. (1) Lightweight "talk to it / plan mode + rules file" users, mostly individual power users. (2) Structured spec-first users, ranging from Harper Reed's spec→plan→execute to GitHub Spec Kit, BMAD, Kiro and Every's plugin. Both camps converge on a persistent repo instruction file (CLAUDE.md / AGENTS.md / copilot-instructions.md) that is updated every time the agent makes a mistake. At companies, the "spec" is often just a Slack thread, ticket or issue handed to a background agent.

### Cited Findings
**Plan mode and rules files**
- [PRACTITIONER/SECONDARY, Boris Cherny (Claude Code creator), 2026-01] Cherny runs five local Claude sessions plus 5–10 on claude.ai. Secondary summaries of his X thread say most sessions start in Plan Mode and switch to auto-accept once the plan is solid. The team keeps one shared CLAUDE.md in git and adds a note whenever Claude makes a mistake. He uses slash commands such as `/commit-push-pr` and subagents such as code-simplifier and verify-app. — [Slashdot/VentureBeat summary](https://developers.slashdot.org/story/26/01/06/2239243/creator-of-claude-code-reveals-his-workflow); original thread x.com/bcherny/status/2007179832300581177 (not directly fetched); [Pragmatic Engineer, 2025-09-23](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-claude-code-is-built)
- [PRACTITIONER, Mitchell Hashimoto, 2026-02-05] Hashimoto calls this "harness engineering": each time an agent errs, he either updates `AGENTS.md` ("implicit prompting") or writes a script or tool (e.g., screenshots, filtered tests) and documents it in AGENTS.md. He says this resolved most bad behaviors in Ghostty. He also separates planning from execution. — [mitchellh.com](https://mitchellh.com/writing/my-ai-adoption-journey)
- [PRACTITIONER, Peter Steinberger, ~Oct 2025] His AGENTS.md is about 800 lines, symlinked to CLAUDE.md, and mostly written by Codex. He calls big upfront specs "the old way" (he used them in June 2025) and now starts with "let's discuss"/"give me options," reserving written specs for tricky features. He calls Claude's plan mode a workaround for the model's eagerness. Prompts are usually 1–2 sentences plus a screenshot, dictated with Wispr Flow. — [steipete.me](https://steipete.me/posts/just-talk-to-it)
- [VENDOR, GitHub, 2025-07-31] For the Copilot coding agent, GitHub recommends well-scoped issues (problem statement, repro steps, suggested approach), `.github/copilot-instructions.md`, path-scoped `.github/instructions/*.instructions.md` with `applyTo` globs, and a `copilot-setup-steps.yml` environment. — [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/onboarding-your-ai-peer-programmer-setting-up-github-copilot-coding-agent-for-success/)
- [VENDOR, GitHub, 2025-10-28] Agent HQ added Plan Mode in VS Code: Copilot asks clarifying questions and builds a plan, then implements it locally or via a cloud agent. It also supports source-controlled AGENTS.md rules and custom agents. — [GitHub](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/welcome-home-agents/)
- [CO-SELF, Stripe, 2026-02-09] Minions read the same rule-file formats as Cursor and Claude Code, mostly applied conditionally by subdirectory. — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents)

**Spec-driven development**
- [PRACTITIONER, Harper Reed, 2025-02-16] Reed's workflow: (1) a chat model asks "one question at a time" to produce `spec.md`; (2) a reasoning model writes `prompt_plan.md` plus `todo.md` with TDD-oriented steps; (3) execution in aider/Cursor/Claude. The plan step takes "maybe 15 minutes." — [harper.blog](https://harper.blog/2025/02/16/my-llm-codegen-workflow-atm/); [Willison summary](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Feb/21/my-llm-codegen-workflow-atm/)
- [PRACTITIONER, Addy Osmani (Google), 2025-12-18] Osmani follows Reed's pattern: brainstorm spec.md, have a reasoning model produce a plan ("waterfall in 15 minutes"), then do small chunks, testing each before moving on. — [Addy Osmani](https://addyo.substack.com/p/my-llm-coding-workflow-going-into)
- [VENDOR, GitHub, 2025-09-02] Spec Kit's `specify` CLI provides `/specify` → `/plan` → `/tasks` → implement, and supported Copilot, Claude Code and Gemini CLI at launch. GitHub frames the shift as "intent is the source of truth." — [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/generative-ai/spec-driven-development-with-ai-get-started-with-a-new-open-source-toolkit/)
- [SECONDARY, 2026] Comparisons characterize Spec Kit as the "heaviest" option, whose branch-per-spec flow can cause "review overload" (one source cites 111k stars, v0.8.7, 30+ agents). Kiro is the "lightest at three markdown files inside the IDE." BMAD is a "portable agile methodology" with role-based agents. Tessl is the only "spec-as-source" tool, marking generated code "DO NOT EDIT" and still in beta. A common practitioner rule of thumb: skip full SDD for anything under a few hundred lines. — [Particula](https://particula.tech/blog/spec-driven-development-tools-spec-kit-vs-kiro-vs-tessl); [SSOJet](https://ssojet.com/blog/best-spec-driven-development-tools); [CodeMySpec — competing vendor](https://codemyspec.com/blog/best-spec-driven-development-tools)
- [PRACTITIONER, Every, 2025-12-11] Every's plan step has the agent produce a document covering objective, architecture, code approach, research sources and success criteria, saved as a file or GitHub issue. — [Every](https://every.to/source-code/compound-engineering-how-every-codes-with-agents). [SECONDARY, 2026-08-24] One report says the open-source Compound Engineering plugin has 33 skills, runs on Claude Code, Cursor and others, and has about 24.5k GitHub stars. — [AI Builder Club](https://www.aibuilderclub.com/blog/compound-engineering-claude-code)
- [PRACTITIONER, Addy Osmani, 2026-05-03, title/summary only] In "Agent Skills" he argues that agents skip specs, tests and reviews unless skills enforce them. His skill pack reportedly ships `/spec /plan /build /test /review /code-simplify /ship`. — [addyosmani.com RSS](https://addyosmani.com/rss.xml); [ai-tldr summary](https://ai-tldr.dev/releases/addyosmani-agent-skills/)

**Issue/ticket-to-PR flows (company standard)**
- [CO-SELF, Stripe, 2026-02-09] Stripe engineers most often launch a minion by tagging it in a Slack thread, so the thread becomes the spec. Minions can also be launched from CLI, web, docs, feature-flag and ticketing UIs, and CI auto-creates flaky-test tickets with a "fix with minion" button. — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents)
- [CO-SELF, Spotify, 2025-11-06] An interactive agent gathers task details from Slack or GitHub Enterprise, converts them into a prompt, and hands it to the background coding agent, which opens a PR. — [Spotify](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2025/11/spotifys-background-coding-agent-part-1)
- [VENDOR/SECONDARY, Factory, 2026] Factory's "Ticket to PR" automation template pulls ready tickets from Linear/Jira, implements them, and opens PRs for review. — [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/factory-s-droid-now-runs-engineering-workflows-autonomously-without-human-input)
- [VENDOR, GitHub, 2025-07-31; docs] Users assign an issue to Copilot like a teammate. It creates a branch and PR, works in a GitHub Actions container, and iterates via PR review comments. — [GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/using-github-copilot/coding-agent/using-copilot-to-work-on-an-issue)

### Inferences
- The "spec" in production company workflows is rarely a formal SDD artifact. It is a ticket, Slack thread or issue plus repo rules files. Formal SDD (Spec Kit/BMAD/Kiro) appears mainly in greenfield or multi-session features and draws criticism for review overload.
- The most universal practice across all sources is the living rules file that grows with each mistake (Cherny, Hashimoto, Steinberger, Every's "compound" step, Huntley's AGENT.md).

### Gaps
- I found no independent data on how widely Spec Kit, Kiro, BMAD, Tessl or task-master are used in production teams, beyond star counts and vendor comparisons. Search budget ran out before I could get task-master or Kiro primary sources.
- I could not read Boris Cherny's original X thread directly. The plan-mode and slash-command details come from secondary summaries.

## Q2. How do engineers run agents in parallel and manage attention?

### Takeaway
Running several agents is now mainstream among power users. Typical counts are 3–8 interactive sessions locally plus some background or cloud agents. The main isolation mechanisms are git worktrees, separate checkouts, or vendor and company cloud sandboxes, and the last of these is what most companies are standardizing on. Human attention, not compute, is the binding constraint: practitioners report review-limited throughput, context-switching cost and fatigue, and several deliberately run only one agent at a time.

### Cited Findings
**Counts**
- [PRACTITIONER, Boris Cherny, 2026-01] Cherny runs 5 Claude Code sessions in numbered terminal tabs with system notifications, plus "5-10 Claudes on claude.ai," handing work between web and local with "teleport." A commenter reports he discards 10–20% of sessions (low confidence). — [Slashdot/VentureBeat](https://developers.slashdot.org/story/26/01/06/2239243/creator-of-claude-code-reveals-his-workflow)
- [PRACTITIONER, Peter Steinberger, ~Oct 2025] Steinberger runs 3–8 Codex agents in a 3x3 terminal grid, mostly in the *same folder*. He tried worktrees and PRs but reverted because the shared folder "gets stuff done the fastest" (shared dev server, OAuth callback limits). Agents make atomic commits of only the files they touched. He dismisses Conductor and Terragon. — [steipete.me](https://steipete.me/posts/just-talk-to-it)
- [PRACTITIONER, Simon Willison, 2025-10-05] Willison runs several agents (Claude Code, Codex CLI, Codex Cloud from his phone) and is experimenting with Copilot coding agent and Jules. He had not adopted worktrees and uses fresh checkouts in /tmp instead. — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/5/parallel-coding-agents/)
- [PRACTITIONER, Mitchell Hashimoto, 2026-02-05] Hashimoto runs *one* background agent, "one at a time, not in parallel": "I'm not [yet?] running multiple agents, and currently don't really want to." A background agent runs for about 10–20% of his working day. — [mitchellh.com](https://mitchellh.com/writing/my-ai-adoption-journey)
- [PRACTITIONER, Addy Osmani, 2025-12-18] Osmani usually runs one main agent plus a secondary agent for reviews and has experimented with 3–4 in parallel. — [Addy Osmani](https://addyo.substack.com/p/my-llm-coding-workflow-going-into)
- [RESEARCH/CO-SELF via SECONDARY, OpenAI paper "The Shift to Agentic AI: Evidence from Codex," arXiv 2606.26959, 2026-06-25] Per secondary summaries, only 10.7% of OpenAI staff ran a single workflow at a time and 28.6% managed five or more concurrent agents during the study week. Codex usage grew more than fivefold in H1 2026, and 70.2% of sampled individual users had at least one request estimated at over 1 hour of human work by May 2026 (model-estimated, "directional"). — [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2026/06/25/codex-study-points-to-works-agentic-ai-future-with-some-surprises.aspx); [Daniel Vaughan summary](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/06/26/openai-shift-to-agentic-ai-evidence-from-codex-usage-data-non-developer-adoption-parallel-agents/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26959)
- [CO-SELF via press, Coinbase, mid-2026] Coinbase's Head of Platform said "most Coinbase engineers now have five to 10 AI agents operating at any given time." This is an executive claim. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/over-95-of-coinbases-code-is-now-written-with-ai)
- [CO-SELF via SECONDARY, Uber, Mar 2026] Uber developers often start a background agent while waiting on another. The summarizer suggests parallelism may reflect slow individual agents rather than a clear efficiency gain. — [ZenML summary of Pragmatic Engineer](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-internal-ai-agent-infrastructure-for-software-development-at-scale)
- [PRACTITIONER, Steve Yegge, 2026-01-01 release] Gas Town orchestrates more than 10 Claude Code agents with roles: Mayor, Polecats (workers in worktrees), Witness, Deacon, and Refinery (merge/quality). State lives in Beads, a git-backed issue tracker. heise reports that even 3–5 parallel agents can produce "chaotic system states," that the tool is alpha, and that scaling needs a 2nd or 3rd Claude Max subscription; heise calls it "not for the faint of heart or the light of wallet." Yegge himself says most developers should not use it. — [heise, 2026-02-21](https://www.heise.de/en/background/Full-Control-Gas-Town-Orchestrates-Ten-or-More-Coding-Agents-11178824.html); [Pragmatic Engineer podcast](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/from-ides-to-ai-agents-with-steve); [SE Daily, 2026-02-12](https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/2026/02/12/gas-town-beads-and-the-rise-of-agentic-development-with-steve-yegge/)

**Tooling for parallelism**
- [VENDOR via SECONDARY, OpenAI, 2026-02-02] The Codex macOS app launched as a multi-agent "command centre" with built-in worktrees and scheduled Automations (issue triage, CI-failure summaries) whose outputs land in a review queue. Cloud-triggered automations were listed as future work. — [CMOtech](https://cmotech.news/story/openai-unveils-codex-macos-app-for-multi-agent-coding)
- [VENDOR, Anthropic, 2025-10-20; GA update dated 2026-09-23] Claude Code on the web runs parallel tasks across repos in isolated sandboxes with network/filesystem restrictions and a git proxy, supports mid-task steering, auto-creates PRs, and has iOS access. Anthropic recommends it for bug fixes, routine well-defined tasks, and backend changes verified with TDD. — [Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/claude-code-on-the-web)
- [VENDOR via SECONDARY changelog aggregators, Cursor, 2026] Cursor's 2026 changelog, per aggregators: long-running agents (Feb 12, 2026); cloud agents in the Agents Window with cloud subagents and runtime options (Cursor-managed / My Machines / Self-Hosted Pool) in v3.7 (June 17); iOS app to launch and steer cloud agents (public beta June 29); Team MCPs (June 30); and cloud-agent "subscriptions" to PRs, Slack threads or schedules plus non-interrupting steering (Aug 19). Dates are approximate and should be checked against cursor.com. — [techdevnotes](https://techdevnotes.com/releases/cursor-ide/20260819-181343Z-621090ca53ad); [gradually.ai](https://www.gradually.ai/changelogs/cursor/)
- [VENDOR, GitHub, 2025-10-28] Agent HQ / "mission control" lets users assign, steer and track multiple agents (Copilot plus third-party Claude, Codex, Jules, Cognition, xAI) across GitHub, VS Code, mobile and CLI, with branch controls over when CI runs on agent code. — [GitHub](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/welcome-home-agents/)
- [SECONDARY, competing vendor, 2026] One roundup says Conductor is Mac-only and proprietary, with worktree-based parallel Claude Code/Codex and good diff review. Claude Squad is an AGPL Go TUI using worktrees. Vibe Kanban's company shut down in April 2026 and the project is now community-maintained. Crystal was deprecated in Feb 2026 in favor of the paid Nimbalyst. Sculptor (Imbue) runs each agent in its own Docker container for stronger isolation, since "worktrees share your machine and environment." — [agentsroom.dev (vendor)](https://agentsroom.dev/blog/best-multi-agent-coding-tools); [Superset (vendor)](https://superset.sh/compare/conductor-alternative)
- [CO-SELF, Stripe, 2026-02-09] Devboxes give "parallelism without git worktrees." Engineers run several minions in parallel during on-call. — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents)
- [CO-SELF, Ramp, 2026-02-19] Inspect supports "hundreds of concurrent sessions," so each builder has "effectively hundreds of computers." — [Modal](https://modal.com/blog/how-ramp-built-a-full-context-background-coding-agent-on-modal)

**Attention management and context switching**
- [PRACTITIONER, Simon Willison, 2025-10-05] "The natural bottleneck on all of this is how fast I can review the results." He reviews and lands only one significant change at a time and parallelizes low-cognitive-overhead work: research, proofs of concept, deprecation-warning fixes, and "scout" runs whose code he never intends to merge. — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/5/parallel-coding-agents/)
- [PRACTITIONER, Mitchell Hashimoto, 2026-02-05] "Turn off agent desktop notifications." He checks agents at natural breaks so he controls interruptions. Cherny takes the opposite approach and uses system notifications. — [mitchellh.com](https://mitchellh.com/writing/my-ai-adoption-journey)
- [SECONDARY/PRACTITIONER, Pragmatic Engineer, 2025-10-30] Armin Ronacher has cut back on parallel agents ("a limit to how much his mind can review"). Gergely Orosz asks whether parallel agents raise productivity "or only create the feeling of it" and notes the flow cost of context switching. — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/new-trend-programming-by-kicking-off-parallel-ai-agents/)
- [PRACTITIONER, Simon Willison via Lenny's Podcast, 2026-04-02] Willison says he writes about 95% of his code from his phone and is "mentally exhausted by 11 a.m." — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/an-ai-state-of-the-union)
- [PRACTITIONER, Addy Osmani, 2026-05-24, title/summary only] "The Orchestration Tax is You": more agents do not add more human attention, because judgment still passes through one person. — [addyosmani.com RSS](https://addyosmani.com/rss.xml)
- [RESEARCH, METR, early 2026] METR reported that time-per-task measurements were "unreliable for developers running multiple agents at once," which was one reason its follow-up study broke down. — [Rob Bowley summary](https://blog.robbowley.net/2026/04/04/metrs-developer-productivity-research-2026-update/)

### Inferences
- Agent counts form a bimodal pattern. Individuals cap at roughly 3–10 interactive sessions, limited by review attention. Companies run hundreds of sandboxed background sessions, limited by reviewer and CI capacity.
- Worktree GUIs (Conductor, Claude Squad, Vibe Kanban, Crystal) appear to be losing ground to built-in vendor features (Codex app worktrees, `claude --worktree`, Cursor Agents Window, Agent HQ) and to cloud sandboxes. Several third-party tools were deprecated or orphaned in 2026.

### Gaps
- I found no independent measurement showing that parallel agents increase *shipped* output per engineer, as opposed to PRs opened. METR's attempt failed for methodological reasons.
- Terragon and Sculptor: no primary sources fetched (search budget exhausted).

## Q3. How do engineers verify agent output?

### Takeaway
The dominant principle is "give the agent a way to verify its own work": tests (often red/green TDD), type checks and linters, browser automation with screenshots, and CI feedback loops. Companies wrap the agent in deterministic pipelines with capped retries and LLM-as-judge vetoes. Re-prompting is still frequent: Anthropic data shows about 4 human turns per session in Aug 2025. Practitioners treat a failed run as something to discard and restart rather than to patch indefinitely.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER, Mitchell Hashimoto, 2026-02-05] Key lesson: "give an agent a way to verify its work." He builds scripts (screenshots, filtered tests) and documents them in AGENTS.md. — [mitchellh.com](https://mitchellh.com/writing/my-ai-adoption-journey)
- [PRACTITIONER, Simon Willison, 2026-02-23] His *Agentic Engineering Patterns* guide has a Testing & QA section covering "Red/green TDD" (write failing tests, confirm they fail, implement), "First run the tests," and "Agentic manual testing" (agents drive browsers and record notes). He names red/green TDD as one of three patterns he uses daily. He lists "Inflicting unreviewed code on collaborators" as an anti-pattern. — [Guide](https://simonwillison.net/guides/agentic-engineering-patterns/); [Red/green TDD](https://simonwillison.net/guides/agentic-engineering-patterns/red-green-tdd/); [Lenny's](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/an-ai-state-of-the-union)
- [PRACTITIONER, Kent Beck, 2025-06-25 and podcast] Beck practices "augmented coding": he enforces TDD, treats the AI as a pair programmer, and refuses shortcuts such as "disabled tests" and unrequested features. - [PRACTITIONER, Every, 2025-12-11] Agents test apps "as a user would" via Playwright or XcodeBuildMCP during the Work step. — [Every](https://every.to/source-code/compound-engineering-how-every-codes-with-agents)
- [PRACTITIONER, Addy Osmani, 2025-12-18] The agent runs tests after each task. Osmani uses Chrome DevTools MCP for runtime debugging and feeds CI, linter and review-bot failures back to the AI. — [Addy Osmani](https://addyo.substack.com/p/my-llm-coding-workflow-going-into)
- [CO-SELF, Stripe, 2026-02-09] Stripe runs lint in under 5 seconds locally, then CI with autofixes. - [CO-SELF, Spotify, 2025-11-06; Part 3 via SECONDARY] Spotify verifies with formatting and linting through MCP tools, then an LLM-as-judge on each diff. - [CO-SELF, Ramp, 2026-02-19] Inspect uses before/after screenshots and real-browser navigation, with Sentry and Datadog integrations. — [Modal](https://modal.com/blog/how-ramp-built-a-full-context-background-coding-agent-on-modal)
- [CO-SELF, Anthropic Engineering, 2026-02-05] In the C-compiler experiment, "the verifier must be nearly perfect, or the agent solves the wrong problem." - [CO-SELF, Shopify, 2025-06-18] Shopify found "allowing AI to roam free around millions of lines of code just didn't work very well." - [CO-SELF, Stripe Part 2 via SECONDARY, 2026-03] "Blueprints" are workflows defined in code that route each subtask either to deterministic routines or to the agent. — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/stripe-autonomous-coding-agents/)

**Re-prompt frequency and success rates**
- [CO-SELF, Anthropic, 2025-12-02] Human turns per Claude Code transcript fell from 6.2 to 4.1 between Feb and Aug 2025. Some respondents reported spending *more* time, mainly debugging Claude's code. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)
- [CO-SELF, Anthropic, Jul 2025, via search summary] Anthropic's RL Engineering team reported autonomous Claude Code tasks succeed on the first attempt about one-third of the time. — [Anthropic case study](https://claude.com/resources/articles/how-anthropic-teams-use-claude-code) (figure seen only in search-result summary)
- [PRACTITIONER, Geoffrey Huntley, 2025-07-14] Huntley's Ralph loop relies on "back pressure" (tests per change, static analysis and type checks) and only one subagent for build/test. When a run degrades he decides whether to `git reset --hard` or write rescue prompts. — [ghuntley.com](https://ghuntley.com/ralph/)

### Inferences
- The verification stack has converged on: rules file → agent-run unit tests/TDD → type checks and linters → browser automation and screenshots → CI with capped retries → AI reviewer or LLM judge → human. The more of this stack a company owns, the more unattended its agents can run (Stripe, Ramp, Spotify).
- "Discard and restart" (fresh context, reset the worktree) is a recognized verification-failure strategy: Ralph, Cursor's judge loop, OpenAI's discard-the-worktree, and Cherny's discarded sessions.

### Gaps
- I found no independent data on how many prompt iterations a typical production PR takes. Anthropic's turns-per-transcript figure is the best proxy and dates from Aug 2025.
- Boris Cherny's widely repeated claim that verification improves quality "2–3x" could not be confirmed from a primary source, so it is excluded.

## Q4. How do teams review AI-written PRs?

### Takeaway
Human review remains the norm and is now the acknowledged bottleneck. Teams respond with (a) layered AI pre-review (multi-agent reviewers, Copilot first-pass review, Claude Code Review, uReview), (b) requiring authors to supply evidence that the change works, (c) small PRs and throttled inflow, and (d) occasionally sampling instead of line-by-line review. Practitioners split on whether review can ever be delegated. Ronacher argues human accountability makes humans the "final bottleneck."

### Cited Findings
- [CO-SELF, Stripe, 2026-02-09] An engineer opens the minion's PR and requests review from *another* engineer. If the result is wrong they send more instructions and the minion pushes an update. — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents)
- [VENDOR via SECONDARY, Anthropic, 2026-03-09] Anthropic launched Code Review for Claude Code: parallel agents scan a PR for different issue types and verify findings against code to cut false positives, then post severity-ranked inline comments (normal / nit / pre-existing). Secondary reports give an average of about 20 minutes and $15–25 per review, and say Anthropic's substantive-comment rate rose from 16% to 54%. Stated motivation: code output per Anthropic engineer "grew 200% in the last year." It is not an auto-approver. — [VKTR](https://www.vktr.com/ai-news/anthropic-launches-multi-agent-code-review-for-claude-code/); [Noqta](https://www.noqta.tn/en/news/claude-code-review-multi-agent-pull-request-review-2026)
- [CO-SELF, Uber, 2025-08-12] uReview was built because "reviewers are overloaded with the increasing volume of code from AI-assisted code development." A secondary summary says it analyzes over 90% of about 65k weekly diffs, with 75% of comments rated useful. — [Uber blog](https://www.uber.com/en-US/blog/ureview/)
- [PRACTITIONER, Every, 2025-12-11] Every's plugin runs 12 review subagents in parallel (security, performance, over-building, etc.). The developer decides which findings to act on, and lessons are "compounded" into rules. — [Every](https://every.to/source-code/compound-engineering-how-every-codes-with-agents)
- [CO-SELF via SECONDARY, OpenAI harness team, 2026] A review agent fires on each PR sync and authors must acknowledge it. Early reviewers "bullied" authors into non-converging loops, so the team biased reviewers toward merging and capped surfaced issues at P2. A 45-minute daily standup exists because agents make large overnight changes that go unnoticed. — [ZenML](https://zenml.io/llmops-database/extreme-harness-engineering-building-production-software-with-zero-human-written-code)
- [PRACTITIONER, Armin Ronacher, 2025-12-22] Ronacher says "code review to me feels like it needs to become part of the VCS." He wants to "see the prompts that led to changes." Local agent review is invisible to teammates, and unreviewed AI open-source PRs are "rage-inducing." — [lucumr](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2025/12/22/a-year-of-vibes/)
- [PRACTITIONER, Armin Ronacher, 2026-02-13] "I too am the bottleneck now." He points to OpenClaw's more than 2,500 open PRs and says AI-first teams "can't triage or review at the pace they generate code." Responses he cites include Pi auto-closing PRs from untrusted contributors and "OSS vacations." — [lucumr](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/2/13/the-final-bottleneck/)
- [PRACTITIONER, Addy Osmani, 2026-01] AI "made the burden of proof explicit": changes should ship with evidence (manual verification plus tests), and review should focus on risk, intent and accountability. He treats AI code like a junior developer's and only merges code he understands. — [Code Review in the Age of AI](https://addyosmani.com/blog/code-review-in-the-age-of-ai/); [workflow post](https://addyo.substack.com/p/my-llm-coding-workflow-going-into)
- [PRACTITIONER, Peter Steinberger, ~Oct 2025] Steinberger reviews selectively (when unsure of the approach), clicks through the UI, and relies on GitHub review bots, `/review`, and `/automerge` on green CI. — [steipete.me](https://steipete.me/posts/just-talk-to-it)
- [RESEARCH, Pragmatic Engineer survey, 2026-03-03, n=906] A common setup is "a terminal running Claude Code to drive the work, with an IDE open to review the agent's changes." Code review and validation is a top agent use case. — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/ai-tooling-2026)
- [SECONDARY/vendor-blog figures, 2026] Faros telemetry: AI usage correlates with 98% more PRs, 154% larger PRs and 91% longer reviews. CircleCI 2026: feature-branch throughput up 59% YoY while main-branch throughput for the median team fell. A cited 2026 study says 61% of AI-agent PRs receive no review. Sonar: 38% say reviewing AI code takes more effort than reviewing a colleague's. These come from vendor blogs and were not verified against the underlying studies. — [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/why-agent-generated-code-is-breaking-the-pull-request-review-model/); [Codacy](https://blog.codacy.com/ai-breaking-code-review-how-engineering-teams-survive-pr-bottleneck); [Votazz](https://votazz.co/2026/07/07/ai-code-review-bottleneck/)
- [CO-SELF, Google Cloud Office of the CTO, 2026] "The bottleneck for shipping software shifted from writing code to reviewing and integrating it." — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/transform/when-ai-writes-the-code-who-reviews-it-cto-google-cloud)
- [CO-SELF, Anthropic, 2025-12-02] Anthropic names a "paradox of supervision": effective oversight requires coding skills that heavy AI use may erode. ### Inferences
- Review is being restructured rather than eliminated. AI reviewers handle breadth (bugs, security, nits) and humans handle intent, architecture and accountability. The open problem, named by Ronacher, Osmani and Anthropic, is that human comprehension does not scale with agent output.
- Teams that report the highest unattended throughput (Stripe, Ramp, Spotify) keep human review but constrain the *task type* to highly verifiable work, which keeps review cheap.

### Gaps
- No primary data on average human review time for agent-authored versus human-authored PRs at a named company.

## Q5. How much of the loop is unattended versus supervised?

### Takeaway
Unattended operation is real but bounded. Common forms are: (1) end-of-day or overnight background runs reviewed the next morning; (2) company background agents that run from trigger to PR without supervision; (3) long-running loop experiments (Ralph, Cursor's planner/worker swarms, Anthropic's 16-agent compiler, OpenAI's harness team, StrongDM) that need heavy up-front harness engineering, large token budgets and human course-correction. Most day-to-day production work is still supervised interactive sessions in which the human steers several times per task.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER, Geoffrey Huntley, 2025-07-14] Ralph is `while :; do cat PROMPT.md | claude-code ; done`: each iteration gets a fresh context and does one task, with state in `specs/*`, `fix_plan.md` and `AGENT.md`. Oversight is substantial: he "tunes Ralph like a guitar," watches the TODO list "like a hawk," and says "engineers are still needed." He calls it "deterministically bad in an undeterministic world," best for greenfield work (about 90% completion), and says "There's no way in heck would I use Ralph in an existing code base." — [ghuntley.com](https://ghuntley.com/ralph/)
- [SECONDARY, 2026] Anthropic released an official Ralph Wiggum plugin for Claude Code, and community forks added spend limits and circuit breakers. Critics say it suits mechanical, well-specified tasks with automatic verification. — [FutureAGI](https://futureagi.com/blog/loop-engineering/ralph-loop/); [Dev Interrupted interview with Huntley](https://devinterrupted.substack.com/p/inventing-the-ralph-wiggum-loop-creator)
- [CO-SELF, Anthropic Engineering, 2026-02-05] 16 parallel Claude agents (Opus 4.6) in Docker ran a bash loop with lockfile task claiming and no orchestrator for about 2 weeks: nearly 2,000 sessions, 2B input and 140M output tokens, just under $20k. The result is a roughly 100k-line Rust C compiler that builds Linux 6.9 on x86/ARM/RISC-V. The human's role was "designing the environment." Limitations: it is not a GCC replacement, and generated code is less efficient than GCC -O0. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)
- [VENDOR, Cursor, 2026-01-14] Cursor uses planners, recursive sub-planners, workers and a judge, with fresh starts each cycle. Hundreds of concurrent agents built a browser (over 1M LoC, about 1 week) and did a Solid→React migration in Cursor's codebase over 3 weeks (+266K/−193K), which "still needs careful review." Failures: locking made 20 agents perform like 2–3, and flat structures made agents "risk-averse." Open problems: drift and the need for "periodic fresh starts." GPT-5.2 outperformed Opus 4.5 on long runs, with Opus "stopping early." — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents)
- [CO-SELF via SECONDARY, OpenAI, 2026-02-11] On the harness-engineering team, 3→7 engineers spent five months building an internal product of over 1M LoC with no manually written code; PRs went from 3–5 to 5–10 per engineer per day after Codex 5.2. The talk summary also notes the first 1.5 months ran about 10x *slower* than human development. — [ZenML](https://zenml.io/llmops-database/extreme-harness-engineering-building-production-software-with-zero-human-written-code); [OpenAI post](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/) (returned 403; not directly verified)
- [CO-SELF via PRACTITIONER, StrongDM, 2026-02-07] StrongDM runs "non-interactive development where specs + scenarios drive agents that write code, run harnesses, and converge without human review." Willison calls the roughly $20k per engineer per month spend "more of a business-model question." — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/7/software-factory/)
- [PRACTITIONER, Mitchell Hashimoto, 2026-02-05] Hashimoto blocks "the last 30 minutes of every day" to start agents on research, exploration and issue triage (report-only, never responding). Most finish in under 30 minutes. The next morning he picks "slam dunks" for background agents. — [mitchellh.com](https://mitchellh.com/writing/my-ai-adoption-journey)
- [CO-SELF, OpenAI forum, 2025-12-04] Romain Huet and Aaron Friel describe "vibe engineering": agents across planning, architecture, debugging and docs, "while keeping humans fully accountable for every line that ships." — [OpenAI Forum](https://forum.openai.com/home/blogs/how-openai-engineers-use-codex-to-tackle-big-projects-with-rigor-2025-12-04) (seen via search summary)
- [CO-SELF, Anthropic, 2025-12-02] Engineers delegate tasks that are easily verifiable, low-stakes, repetitive, well-defined or outside their expertise. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)
- [VENDOR via SECONDARY, Cursor, 2026-07/08] Cursor's always-on cloud agents "build, test, and ship code while you keep working." Agents can subscribe to PR, Slack or schedule events. — [techdevnotes](https://techdevnotes.com/releases/cursor-ide/20260819-181343Z-621090ca53ad)
- [VENDOR via SECONDARY, Factory, 2026] Factory Custom Automations run Droids on schedules or triggers with chosen models and machines. — [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/factory-s-droid-now-runs-engineering-workflows-autonomously-without-human-input)
- [SECONDARY, 2026] Claude Code's `/loop` reportedly runs recurring local tasks for up to three days and `/schedule` runs cloud jobs after the laptop closes. This comes only from an unattributed secondary summary of Cherny's workflow.

### Inferences
- Long-running autonomy is economically and technically gated. Showcase runs cost $20k (Anthropic compiler) up to "trillions of tokens" (Cursor), and all of them needed near-perfect verifiers. For typical product teams the practical form of "unattended" is the bounded background agent: one ticket → one PR, capped CI retries, human review.
- Vendors raced through 2026 to productize scheduling and event triggers (Codex Automations, Cursor subscriptions, Factory automations, Claude Code routines). This suggests demand is moving from "chat with an agent" to "agents that wake up on events."

### Gaps
- The Huntley overnight anecdotes (CURSED compiler overnight; $50k contract for $297) are self-reported or secondhand. The CURSED repo is "full of garbage" by his own account.
- I did not find independent data on what share of company agent runs are unattended versus interactive.

## Q7. What do company engineering blogs say about their AI-agent workflow?

### Takeaway
Large companies have converged on building internal background-agent platforms. Examples: Stripe Minions (a Goose fork), Ramp Inspect (OpenCode on Modal), Spotify Honk, Uber Minion, and Shopify Roast for structured workflows. Each wraps a commodity agent in pre-warmed sandboxes, internal tool access via MCP, deterministic pre and post steps, CI loops and mandatory human PR review, and is triggered from Slack, tickets or CI. Reported shares of merged PRs produced by agents range from about 11% (Uber, Mar 2026) to over 50% (Ramp), and "AI-written/assisted code" claims run as high as 80–95% (Anthropic, Coinbase). All of these are self-reported.

### Cited Findings
- **Anthropic** [CO-SELF]: On 2025-12-02 (Aug 2025 data, n=132 surveyed, 53 interviewed, 200k transcripts), Claude was used in 59% of work, up from 28%. Self-reported productivity was +50%, up from +20%. Merged PRs per engineer per day rose 67% after Claude Code adoption, and 27% of Claude-assisted work "would not have been done otherwise." ### Inferences
- The company pattern is "build a harness, not an agent." Stripe, Ramp and Spotify all treat the model and agent as swappable (Goose fork, OpenCode, internal CLI) and invest in the environment: pre-warmed sandboxes, internal tool access, fast tests, CI, and Slack/ticket triggers.
- Background agents succeed first on migrations, flaky tests, config changes and small well-defined fixes. These are the categories where the verification oracle is strong and review is cheap.
- Headline "% of code written by AI" numbers (Anthropic 80%+, Coinbase 95%) measure authorship, not autonomy or quality, and are not comparable across companies.
